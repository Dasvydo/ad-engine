# The operating loop

What to ask for, how often, and what the answer means on an account with no
conversion events.

---

## Every session, first question

> Pull frequency, reach, spend and CPM for the last 7 days at ad set level.

**Frequency is the number that matters.** Everything else is context for it.

| Frequency (7d) | Reading | Do |
|---|---|---|
| under 2 | healthy | nothing |
| 2–3 | working as intended | nothing |
| 3–4 | wearing out | rotate to the other creative arm |
| over 4 | burning the list | cut budget or pause; the audience has seen it |

Why: ~2,000 people is a small pool. Meta will keep buying impressions after
everyone has already seen the ad, because nothing tells it to stop. You are the
thing that tells it to stop.

---

## Weekly

> Compare this week to last: spend, reach, frequency, CPM, link clicks, cost per
> link click. Flag anything that moved more than 30%. Do not speculate about
> causes you cannot see in the data.

**Read CPM as a market signal, not a performance one.** Rising CPM on a fixed
audience usually means more advertisers bidding for the same people, or the
audience is exhausted. Neither is something your creative fixed or broke.

**Do not read week-to-week lead counts as signal.** At this budget you will get
single digits. Four leads one week and one the next is noise, not a 75% drop.
Log it, do not act on it.

---

## Monthly

> Which of the two creative arms — `capacity` or `hours` — carried more spend and
> more link clicks? Present both, state plainly that the sample is too small to
> call a winner unless it obviously is.

The A/B is the point of running at all: `capacity` framing is
**reasoned but untested**, and `docs/ICP-BRIEF.md` names it the highest-value
thing to test. Meta is one of two places testing it; outreach-engine is the other,
running the same two arms. Read them together.

---

## What "winning" means here

Not ROAS. Not cost per lead. There is not enough volume for either.

**The honest measure is whether outreach performs better into firms that saw the
ads.** If you held some firms out of the custom audience, compare reply rates
between the two groups. That is a real test and it is the only one this channel
can pass or fail.

⚠️ A holdout shrinks the audience. 20% of 750 firms held back leaves ~1,600
people — still over Meta's 1,000 floor, but the margin is thin. Check the built
file's row count before deciding.

---

## Scheduled reports

The one mechanism from the video course that ports here unchanged. Worth setting
up; it costs nothing to run.

- **Daily** is too often for this spend. Weekly is right.
- Give it the business context or the analysis will be worthless — it will call a
  deliberately tiny budget "underperforming" because it has no idea the audience
  is 2,000 people.
- Anything it flags is a prompt to look, never an instruction to act.
